What members see today

The page isn't broken. It's built for people who run forums, not for photographers who want to post a picture and hear back. Of the ~60 controls, about a dozen matter to a typical member, and the one thing they'd look for (am I a member, until when?) isn't there.

A "My account" page as the front door

One page, five cards, written in the club's words. Each card answers one question a member actually has: am I a member, how do others see me, how much email will I get, which language, and how do I leave or take my data. The full Discourse preferences stay behind a single "All settings" link.

How it's built

  • Same pattern as /profile-setup, which already works: a theme route at /my-account, a permalink so a hard reload lands there, and an avatar-menu item. The avatar menu's existing "Preferences" entry and your own /u/<you>/preferences index redirect to it, with the same ?full=1 escape hatch and an on/off setting (my_account_replaces_preferences) so the takeover can be switched off live.
  • Every card reads and writes what Discourse already stores. Profile completeness comes from the fields /profile-setup edits. Email presets and language save through PUT /u/<username>.json, the endpoint /profile-setup uses. Export and account closure link to the existing actions (closure is spc-account-closure.gjs, today rendered in the Account tab).
  • "All settings" opens the native Account tab with ?full=1. Nothing native is hidden or rewritten, which keeps us clear of the "never decorate a DOM element core owns" trap.
  • All copy goes into locales/{en,de,fr}.yml under a new my_account.* namespace.
Profile and "My account" stay separate pages on purpose: Profile is what other members see, My account is private. Merging them would make /profile-setup long again, which is the problem it solved.

Step 1 — status only, theme-only, ships immediately

  • Discourse already knows the status: group 40 members and group 44 trial. The card resolves it exactly like the non-member banner does (nonmember_member_groups, Object.hasOwn fast path with the currentUser.groups fallback).
  • "Become a member" reuses nonmember_join_url_{de,en,fr}. "Manage on swissphotoclub.com" needs one new URL setting per language, pointing at the account page where plans are already managed.
  • Without dates the member line reads "Active member" and the trial line reads "Trial member", which already answers the question.

Step 2 — plan name and end date, from spc-be

  • spc-be already computes all of it: CommunityAccessSummary has label(), endsAt(), isOpenEnded() and isTrialOnly(). Discourse just never receives it.
  • Add two fields to the payload in DiscourseSsoPayloadFactory::payload(): custom.spc_plan and custom.spc_access_until. DiscourseConnect stores custom.* keys as user custom fields. Push them from SyncDiscourseUserJob too, so a renewal shows up without a new login.
  • Decide who may see them. Discourse only sends user custom fields to the browser through public_user_custom_fields (every visitor can read them) or staff_user_custom_fields (staff only). Showing a member their own plan and end date, and nobody else, needs a few lines of server code: a tiny plugin that adds the two fields to the current-user serializer. That's one more entry in containers/app.yml and a rebuild.
The date is only as fresh as the last sync. The reconcile job (DISCOURSE_RECONCILE_ENABLED) was suspected not to be running in production in August; check it before showing dates, or a lapsed trial will keep showing "12 days left".

4

Say what can't be changed here, and where to change it

The site signs members in through their Swiss Photo Club account (DiscourseConnect). That's why the email has no edit button and there's no password field anywhere. Discourse doesn't say so, and members reasonably think something is broken.

Email and passwordShow "From your Swiss Photo Club account" next to the email, with a "Change on swissphotoclub.com" link. Same link in the Security area for password changes.
Namespc-be sends the name with every sign-in, but auth_overrides_name is off, so a change made here sticks. Say "Shown on your posts" so members know what it's for.
UsernameChanging it renames the @mention handle and breaks old mentions. Keep it on the native page only (All settings), not on My account.
TitleOnly matters for staff and badge holders. Keep it native too.
5

Get the defaults right

Most members will never open any settings page, however good it is. What they get by default is the real settings experience. Today's site defaults are reasonable, with two gaps.

Already goodReplies and mentions email only when away (default_email_level = 1), a weekly summary (default_email_digest_frequency = 10080), and first-post notifications for Club News (5) and Live Webinars (13).
Gap: challenge rounds and resultsNothing tells members a new round opened or who won, which is part of Stefan's complaint about results. Once the challenge-news tag exists, add it to default_tags_watching_first_post. That's the default half of the switch in recommendation 3.
Website Feedback is muted for everyonedefault_categories_muted = 2. Fine for keeping the inbox quiet, but members who post feedback won't hear about replies to other people's ideas. Probably intended; worth a deliberate yes.
Apply new defaults to existing membersDiscourse's "Update existing users" checkbox applies a changed default to everyone. Use it for the challenge tag; don't use it for email levels, which some members have set on purpose.
